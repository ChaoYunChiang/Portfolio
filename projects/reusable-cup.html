Reusable Cup Market Research

A marketing study of the PlastiCircular Cup, a reusable cup service at FamilyMart.

Role
Survey design and strategy
Time
Spring 2025
Type
Market research, class project
Team
Group of six

Challenge

Customers can borrow and return the cup at FamilyMart, yet only about 1% of the people we surveyed had used the service.

Bring their own bottle or cup, sometimes or often
79%
Buy a non-bottled drink at least once a week
81%
Know the cup service and have used it
1.1%

Research

We ran a 20-question survey from April 18 to May 8 and received 97 responses. I designed the survey. It covers five parts: background, habits, attitudes, promotion, and environmental awareness. It starts with background questions for cross-analysis, screens for people who bring their own cups or know the service, then asks follow-up questions with skip logic.

FindingWhat the survey showed
ProductFocus
No distinctive features, poor insulation, hygiene concerns		45.7% of non-users cite hygiene. About 80% rate hygiene 3 out of 5 or lower.
ConvenienceFocus
A complicated process, scattered locations, little information		35.8% do not know where to rent or return. About 80% rate convenience 3 or lower.
Habit
No incentive, not a necessity		70.4% of non-users say they have no habit.
MarketingFocus
Low awareness, limited channels		57.7% did not know the service existed.
Say versus doAbout 80% carry their own bottle or cup, yet 51.6% do not bring a cup to buy drinks.

We focused on the three highlighted findings. The survey is a class sample of 97 responses, not a representative one.

Strategy

The goal was to raise real usage from 1% to 5% or more, build trust in the cup, and close the gap between what people say and what they do. I wrote the strategy with three directions.

DirectionPlan
Make it easySelf-service kiosks near campuses and commute hubs. A live cup map in the app.
Give a reasonApp points, levels, a leaderboard and a 7-day challenge. Co-branded cup designs.
Spread the wordA social challenge, "Talker or doer?", an outfit challenge for bottles, and reviews from KOLs and KOCs.

A class project for a market research course. The cup service and its brands belong to their owners, and this study is not affiliated with them.
